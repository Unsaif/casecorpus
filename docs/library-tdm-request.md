# Draft: text-and-data-mining access request to the University of Galway Library

Subject: Enabling publisher text-and-data-mining (TDM) access for a rare-disease research corpus

Dear [Research Services / E-resources team],

I am a PhD student in Prof. Ines Thiele's group (metabolic modelling; rare disease diagnosis). We are
building a research resource that extracts structured, individual-level clinical, biochemical and
genetic information from published case reports of inherited metabolic disorders (about 42,000
PubMed records; roughly 13 % have open-access full text). The remainder sits largely in journals the
University subscribes to — Molecular Genetics and Metabolism (Elsevier), the Journal of Inherited
Metabolic Disease, JIMD Reports and the American Journal of Medical Genetics (Wiley), and the
European Journal of Pediatrics (Springer Nature), among others.

We would like to access this content programmatically for non-commercial research text and data
mining under Article 3 of Directive (EU) 2019/790 (the research TDM exception, transposed in Ireland
by S.I. No. 567/2021), through the publishers' sanctioned TDM channels rather than by any bulk
download through the proxy:

- Elsevier: institutional token for the ScienceDirect Article Retrieval API (X-ELS-Insttoken) with
  a TDM-enabled API key;
- Wiley: TDM client token via Crossref's TDM click-through service / Wiley's TDM API;
- Springer Nature: TDM-enabled API key.

Full text will be stored only on university-controlled systems, used solely to derive structured
facts and short quotations (a few hundred characters) that support them, and never redistributed.
Extracted records will be released under an open licence with citation of each source article.
We are happy to sign any publisher TDM agreement the library requires and to provide a data
management plan.

Could you let us know the process and any forms needed for each publisher?

With thanks,
